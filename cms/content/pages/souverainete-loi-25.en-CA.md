## Read this first.

“Law 25” is the name given to the 2021 law that modernized privacy rules in Quebec. For a private business, those rules are gathered in the [Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/p-39.1).

This guide summarizes what those texts provide, as we read them on the official sites. It may contain mistakes or be out of date. For your own situation, consult a lawyer or Quebec's Commission d'accès à l'information.

- **This guide informs.** It explains obligations in plain language. Article numbers are landmarks: only the text of the law is authoritative.
- **Nexora does not make you compliant.** No software and no supplier does. Protecting personal information remains the responsibility of the business that holds it.
- **Nexora does not claim compliance either.** We claim no attestation or label on this subject. We describe what we provide, and what is still in preparation.

## What the law requires of a business.

The law applies to anyone who operates a business in Quebec. These are the obligations that most directly affect an AI project.

#### A designated officer (s. 3.1).

By default, the person with the highest authority in the business; the role can be delegated in writing. Their title and contact details must be published on the business's website.

#### Published governance rules (s. 3.2).

Retention and destruction of information, staff roles, complaint handling. These rules are published in plain terms on the website.

#### An assessment before the project (s. 3.3).

Any project to acquire, develop or redesign an information system that involves personal information requires a privacy impact assessment (EFVP in French), proportionate to how sensitive the information is. The officer is consulted from the start.

#### Supervision of suppliers (s. 18.3).

When a supplier receives information to carry out a mandate or a service contract, a written contract must set protective measures, limit use to that mandate and require notice of any breach.

#### A framework for transfers outside Quebec (s. 17).

Before sending information outside Quebec, an assessment is required. The transfer can go ahead if the assessment shows adequate protection, and it is covered by a written agreement that takes the assessment into account.

#### Incidents handled (s. 3.5 to 3.8).

Steps to limit harm, notice to the Commission and to the people affected when the risk of serious harm is real, and a register of all incidents.

#### People informed (s. 8 and 8.1).

Information at the time of collection; information and activation options when a technology can identify, locate or profile a person.

#### Automated decisions explained (s. 12.1).

When a decision rests exclusively on automated processing, the person is told, can learn the reasons and main factors, and can submit comments to someone able to review the decision.

#### Rights to respect (s. 27 and following).

Access and correction: anyone can ask for their information; the business must respond within the time the law sets.

#### Possible penalties.

The law provides for administrative monetary penalties and penal sanctions. The amounts are in the text of the law.

## Where AI meets the law.

An AI that reads your emails, invoices or customer files processes personal information. These are the five moments when the obligations above most often come up.

- **Choosing the system.** This is when the privacy impact assessment happens: which information, for what purpose, where, and who can access it.
- **Choosing a model.** A model that runs outside Quebec is a transfer outside Quebec: prior assessment and a written agreement. A model on your own server avoids that question.
- **Handing over hosting or operations.** A supplier that hosts or runs the system for you receives information: the written contract and its protective measures apply.
- **Letting the system decide.** As soon as a decision about a person rests solely on automated processing, information and the possibility of review are required.
- **Handling an incident.** You need to know who assesses, who notifies and where the incident is recorded. Better to decide before one happens.

## What stays with your business.

Nexora can supply technical information and tools. These responsibilities remain yours.

#### Designate and publish.

Name the officer responsible for protecting personal information, publish their title and contact details, and publish your governance rules.

#### Assess.

Carry out the privacy impact assessment of your project. We can describe the technical aspects; the conclusion and the decision are yours.

#### Contract.

Sign the written agreements required with your suppliers, Nexora included, and with any service located outside Quebec.

#### Inform.

Tell your customers, employees and prospects what you collect, why, and whether a technology identifies or profiles them.

#### Respond.

Handle access, correction and complaint requests within the time limits set by the law.

#### Record.

Keep the incident register and decide, with your officer, what must be reported.

#### Seek advice.

Have your approach validated by a lawyer or a privacy adviser.

## Law 25: your questions.

#### Does my small business fall under it?

The law applies to anyone who operates a business in Quebec and holds personal information. If you process information about customers, employees or suppliers, it concerns you.

#### Does hosting in Quebec settle the matter?

Hosting in Quebec can simplify certain points; transfers outside Quebec must be assessed with your legal adviser.

#### Must I tell my customers that I use AI?

It depends on what the system does: collection, profiling, automated decisions. The information rules are described above. The exact answer for your case belongs to your officer and your legal adviser.

#### Is Nexora compliant?

We do not put it that way: compliance is not a state a supplier awards itself. We describe what we document, what the software allows and what is in preparation.
