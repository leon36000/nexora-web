## What Nexora Vision does not do.

Trustworthy surveillance is defined as much by what it refuses as by what it does. Here are our refusals, written down in advance.

- **No facial recognition.** This function is not delivered. Identifying people would require a declaration to the Commission d'accès à l'information, express consent and a non-biometric alternative: we do not offer it.
- **No employee analytics.** No productivity or behaviour measurement. Alerts are limited to the zones and hours you define.
- **No identity, no intent.** The AI detects people and objects. It does not say who someone is, or what they intend to do.
- **No training on your footage.** Your footage is never used to train or fine-tune a model, ours or a third party's.
- **No replacement for a security guard.** An alert calls for a person to check it. It does not replace an intervention.
- **No invented figure.** No detection rate, false-alert rate or drop in theft is announced: the pilot will measure them, and we will publish the method.

## Where to start: your risk sites.

Studies of video surveillance show a real but modest effect on crime, clearer in parking lots and stronger when someone watches actively. The gain does not come from AI alone, which is why the pilot starts where the evidence is strongest.

#### Parking lots.

People and vehicles outside opening hours, intrusion alerts. This is the ground where video surveillance has proven itself.

#### Warehouses and loading docks.

Perimeter, docks, access outside working hours. Large video flows: the hardware is sized with you.

#### Construction sites.

Detecting a presence outside working hours near stored equipment. Connectivity and power on site are checked first.

#### Shops.

Entrances, restricted zones, back rooms. No tracking of customers from one aisle to the next.

#### Offices and server rooms.

Access outside working hours and restricted areas. Never a camera pointed at workstations.

#### Clinics and restaurants: prior study.

Places with a strong expectation of privacy, or where staff would be filmed continuously: we proceed only after a legal analysis with you.

## Law 25, plainly.

Filming people means processing personal information. Quebec's private-sector privacy law (Law 25) applies, and **your business** is responsible for it. Here is what it asks, in plain words. No software or supplier can, on its own, bring a business into line with this law.

This is not legal advice. Consult a lawyer before any installation, especially in a work area.

- **Inform before filming.** People must know they are being filmed before they enter: a sign at the entrance, with a contact.
- **Write the rule.** A video surveillance policy says why, where, when and for how long you film.
- **Assess before deploying.** A privacy impact assessment weighs the risks before installation.
- **Limit and destroy.** Keep only what is useful and destroy the rest once the purpose is met.
- **What Nexora AI prepares with you.** An assessment, a policy and a model sign delivered before installation. These documents are in preparation.

## Your questions. Our answers.

#### Does my footage leave my premises?

No, that is the principle we aim for: the analysis runs on the server installed at your site. Only written alerts (type of object, time, zone) can leave, and only if you switch them on. Updates and any licences are listed before installation. As no site is installed yet, we will verify it with a test during the pilot before stating it as fact.

#### Do you do facial recognition?

No. This function is not delivered. Identifying people would require a declaration to the Commission d'accès à l'information, express consent and a non-biometric alternative: we do not offer it.

#### Is my footage used to train an AI?

Never. We do not use your footage to train or fine-tune any model, ours or a third party's. It is one of our five commitments on your data.

#### Must I inform my employees and customers?

Yes. Quebec law asks that people be informed before collection, and a sign at the entrance is the recommended practice. We prepare a model sign and a policy with you; these documents are in preparation and do not replace legal advice.

#### How long is the footage kept?

You choose, by type of event. The footage is then destroyed automatically. This is the design aimed for in the pilot.

#### Can I use my current cameras?

Often, yes. We first check compatibility with the common standards (ONVIF and RTSP) before installation. Some cameras will not be compatible.

#### Is it legal to monitor my employees?

It depends on the reason, the zones and the hours. Continuous monitoring of staff or of their productivity is risky and can be refused by an arbitrator. We do not offer that function. Consult a lawyer before any installation in a work area.

#### What happens if the AI gets it wrong?

Every alert is checked by a person and recorded in the log. The pilot measures the false-alert rate on your site. An alert does not replace a security intervention, and the contract sets out the limits of liability.

#### How much does it cost?

There is no public price yet. It will depend on the number of cameras, the storage and the retention period; a written estimate follows the diagnostic.

#### How do I join the pilot?

Describe your site: type of place, approximate number of cameras, reason for the surveillance. We reply to see whether your case fits the pilot.
